**Title:** Learning in Feedback Systems: Stability, Optimization, and Adaptation.

**Abstract:**
Learning-based controllers offer remarkable flexibility, but their deployment in feedback systems raises fundamental questions about stability, convergence, and robustness. This talk presents a control-theoretic perspective on three complementary learning paradigms: neural-network feedback, policy optimization and reinforcement learning, and adaptive control. First, I discuss mathematical tools for certifying the stability and robustness of feedback systems containing neural-network controllers, with particular attention to positive systems and regions of attraction. I then examine the optimization dynamics underlying policy learning, highlighting recent results on the almost-global exponential convergence of gradient flow for overparameterized linear-quadratic regulator policies. These results reveal how policy parameterization and optimization geometry influence closed-loop learning. Finally, I compare reinforcement learning with adaptive control under model uncertainty and communication constraints, and present distributed adaptive architectures for uncertain networked systems, including consensus under intermittent communication. Together, these results illustrate how control theory can provide rigorous guarantees for learning in feedback systems while clarifying the complementary roles of data-driven optimization and structured online adaptation.
